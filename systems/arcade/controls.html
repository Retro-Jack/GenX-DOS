Arcade — Controls

A hundred coin-op cabinets from ten manufacturers, in your browser. Unlike a home machine, every cabinet here had its own control panel — a different stick, a different number of buttons, sometimes a trackball or a spinner — so this page covers what is the same everywhere and each game's own page covers what is not. Click the game area to enable sound and controls.

The control panel

Six buttons, because these cabinets used them — Defender alone needs five. What each button does is different on every machine, so the numbers above are deliberately unlabelled; the game's own page names them. They are laid out by how often a button gets pressed against how far the hand has to move, which is why Button 2 sits under your index finger on RB: on the cabinets that use it, it is usually held down.

Coins and starting

GamepadKeyboardAction
on-screen coin slot5Insert coin
on-screen 1P button11 player start
on-screen 2P button22 player start

Every cabinet needs a credit before it will start: insert a coin first, then press start. The three buttons at the top left of the cabinet do the same thing as the keys, so a gamepad player can reach them without a keyboard. They are deliberately not on the pad — on a coin-op, a stray press of Back would quietly rack up credits mid-game.

Cabinets with two sticks

Five machines here had a stick for each hand, and they are wired to the pad's two sticks accordingly. Robotron: 2084 and Splat! move with the left and fire with the right; Battlezone and Sheriff drive a tank tread with each; Inferno moves with one and aims with the other.

GamepadKeyboardAction
Left stick← → ↑ ↓First stick — usually move
Right stickJ L I KSecond stick — usually fire or aim

On the ninety-four games that are not one of those five and are not Tutankham the second stick does nothing, and the arrow keys drive the D-pad and the left stick alike — so movement is the arrow keys everywhere, whichever machine you are on.

Cabinets whose buttons were a place, not a number

The pad's X, Y and B sit in a diamond — X on the left, B on the right, Y above between them — and three of these panels were laid out in space rather than as a row of numbered buttons. On those the diamond is the panel.

GamepadKeyboardAction
X / Y / BZ V XGun.Smoke — fire down the left, the centre or the right of the street
X / Y / BZ V XMissile Command — fire from the left, centre or right missile base
X / B, YZ X, VPunch-Out!! — left blow, right blow, and the uppercut above them

Gun.Smoke fires diagonally when two are pressed together, so a diagonal shot is the two buttons either side of the one you want.

The cabinet that aims along a corridor

Tutankham fires left or right down the passage the explorer is standing in, and nothing else — so it is the right stick rather than a pair of buttons. Push it the way you want the shot to go. The Flash bomb stays on X.

Right stick ← →Z XFire left / fire right

Cabinets that were not joysticks

Twenty-one of these machines had something else on the panel: a trackball on Centipede, Missile Command and Marble Madness, a spinner on Tempest and Arkanoid, a yoke on Star Wars, a wheel on Pole Position and Out Run. They all play here, but from the D-pad or the keyboard the control is digital rather than proportional — hold a direction and it moves at a fixed rate, where the real cabinet moved as fast as you spun or rolled it. Hold the direction rather than tapping it; a tap is often too short for the machine to see at all.

Star Wars is the exception, with a gamepad. Its yoke is a true analogue control and the left stick drives it as one — how far you push it is how far the crosshair goes, and letting go returns it to the centre, exactly as the sprung yoke did. It is the only cabinet here where the stick beats the D-pad outright.

The save / load controls in the bottom-left corner each open a menu of five save slots — a full machine snapshot per slot, so they work mid-game on cabinets that never had a save of their own. Slots are per-game and survive a reload. To restart, reload the page.
