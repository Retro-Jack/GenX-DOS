Repton Infinity

Superior Software, 1988 — Dave Lawrence & Andrew Stubbs. The series finale: five distinct sub-games plus a full programming language ("REPTON Infinity Language") for designing your own games.

The "Infinity" in the name is literal: as well as 5 ready-made sub-games (Repton, Robbo, Trakka, Bopper, Doopin), the disc includes an editor and a Pascal-flavoured language for writing entirely new Repton-style games. Most players never touch the language — the five included games are themselves a full Repton package.

Getting started

  1. Press Space at the title to enter the sub-game selector.
  2. Pick a game with A–E: Repton (classic), Robbo (robot collector), Trakka (track-laying puzzle), Bopper (jumping puzzle), Doopin (multi-character co-op).
  3. Each sub-game has its own screen set and rules; the level editor on the disc menu loads either built-in or saved screens.

The five sub-games

  • Repton — classic format: collect diamonds and crowns, avoid boulders and monsters.
  • Robbo — robot pushing objects in a constrained grid; more puzzle than reflex.
  • Trakka — lay track pieces ahead of a moving train; a precursor to the design language.
  • Bopper — jumping platformer where Repton bounces between pads.
  • Doopin — control multiple characters to solve interlocked rooms.

Strategy

  • Start with Repton sub-game. It maps onto Repton 3 muscle memory; the others diverge sharply.
  • Doopin needs planning. Each character is controlled in turn — keep a mental queue of who's needed where.
  • Save your designs. The editor commits to disc and you can swap screens via the cross-import menu — but disc writes don't survive closing the tab, so use the load/save buttons at the bottom-left to keep your work. There is only one save slot — it's the best we could do.
  • The Infinity Language isn't required to enjoy the included content — but it does mean the disc effectively never runs out of new content if a community shares level packs.
