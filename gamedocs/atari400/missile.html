Missile Command

Atari, 1981 — home port of Dave Theurer's 1980 arcade. Authored during the Cold War; Theurer is on record about the nuclear-war anxiety dreams the development induced. The Atari 8-bit version replaces the arcade's trackball with a digital joystick crosshair.

Defend six cities and three missile bases against waves of incoming nuclear warheads. Fire counter-missiles to detonate them mid-air. Each city saved at wave-end pays bonus points; lose all six and the planet is destroyed.

Getting started

  1. Press F4 Start.
  2. Move the targeting cross-hair with the joystick.
  3. Fire counter-missiles with L Ctrl — they detonate at the cursor.
  4. Defend cities and reload bases.

Strategy

  • Lead the missile. Counter-missiles take ~0.5 sec to reach the cursor — fire at the path the incoming warhead will follow, not where it is.
  • Big detonations chain. One well-placed explosion can engulf multiple incoming missiles at once.
  • Kill MIRVs before split. Each MIRV becomes 3 separate ICBMs after splitting.
  • Sacrifice cities strategically. If two cities are dead and the third is the bonus pivot, prioritise it.
  • Manage three bases. Each base has 10 missiles; if a base is destroyed you lose its ammo. Fire from the closest base to spread depletion.
  • Bonus city at 10,000. Each multiple of 10,000 points awards a replacement city.

Atari 400 controls

Joystick + fire (the original arcade used a trackball; the digital crosshair is slower but still playable). Some Atari 8-bit ports support analogue paddle for cursor control via port 4 — the cart in our bundle uses standard joystick.
