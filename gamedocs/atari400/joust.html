Joust

Atari, 1983 — home port of John Newcomer's 1982 Williams arcade. You ride a flying ostrich (or stork in 2-player) and joust against enemy knights riding buzzards across floating platforms. Single flap-button control; one of the most pure-mechanic arcade titles ever made.

Defeat enemy knights by being higher than them when your lances cross. When a knight is unhorsed, their egg falls to the platform — collect it before it hatches into another knight. Survive waves; the Pterodactyl and Lava Troll add complications later.

Getting started

  1. Press F4 Start.
  2. Tap L Ctrl repeatedly to flap upward.
  3. Steer with the joystick left/right.
  4. Charge at knights — higher lance wins the collision.

Strategy

  • Flap higher. Lance height decides every joust — climb above the enemy first, then charge.
  • Egg collection. When you unhorse a knight, the egg drops and slides. Grab it before it hatches into a respawned knight.
  • Pterodactyl beak. The Pterodactyl screeches across the screen. Lance into its open beak; any other contact kills you.
  • Avoid the lava. The bottom strip is lava. A flap from the lava surface costs more energy than from solid ground.
  • 2-player co-op or vs. Two ostriches can also joust each other — friendly fire is on by default.
  • Wave 5 = Egg Wave. All eggs hatch simultaneously; sweep the platforms before they all turn into knights.

Atari 400 controls

Joystick + fire (flap). One button + one axis is the whole game — Joust is famously the purest arcade-control game on the platform.
