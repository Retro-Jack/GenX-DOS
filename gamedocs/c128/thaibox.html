Thai Boxing

Anco, 1986. A one-on-one Muay Thai fighter shown in an isometric ring. This is the native Commodore 128 build — it uses the machine's extra memory for digitized speech and animated ringside spectators (an elder man and his dog) that the C64 release doesn't have.

You face a single opponent across three rounds. Different joystick directions combined with fire throw kicks and punches at three heights — high, middle and low — while the same stick handles stepping in and out, blocking and dodging. Land the harder strikes for more points; win by knockout, or by holding the higher score when the final round ends.

Getting started

  1. The .d64 autoboots into C128 mode; click the canvas first to give it focus.
  2. Press L Ctrl (fire) at the title to begin a bout.
  3. The disk has an on-screen INSTRUCTIONS option that lists every move — worth a look, as this is a combo-driven fighter.

Controls

The game is joystick-driven; this emulator maps the stick to the arrow keys (the numeric keypad works too) and fire to L Ctrl. A USB gamepad also works. Attacks are direction-plus-fire combinations that pick the strike and its height.

InputAction
← / →Step in / out (turn to face)
↑ / ↓Guard / crouch
L CtrlAttack
L Ctrl + directionHigh / middle / low kicks and punches, blocks and dodges

Strategy

  • Vary the height. Mixing high, middle and low attacks stops the opponent settling into one block — a predictable attacker gets countered.
  • Points reward difficulty. The showier, harder-to-land strikes score more, so a clean knockout isn't the only route — you can win on points over the three rounds.
  • Block and dodge, don't just trade. Defence is on the same stick; reading the incoming height and guarding beats swinging back every time.
