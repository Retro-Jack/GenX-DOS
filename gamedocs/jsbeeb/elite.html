Elite

Acornsoft, 1984 — David Braben & Ian Bell. The first open-world space trading game, with 2 048 procedurally-generated planets across 8 galaxies, fitted into 22 KB of a BBC Model B. Disc version (this build) adds the docking computer and mission content.

You start in Lave with 100 credits, a Cobra Mk III, a pulse laser, and a rating of "Harmless". Trade goods between systems, fight (or flee) the pirates that ambush you in hyperspace, dock at space stations, upgrade your ship — and over hundreds of hours of play try to reach the "Elite" combat rating with 6 400 kills.

Getting started

  1. At the title press Space, then 2 to start a new commander (or 1 to load).
  2. You launch from inside Lave station — match the rotation of the stars before flying out, then hit J to in-system jump to the planet.
  3. Press 7 for the market, buy Food (cheap on Lave), 5/6 for the chart, F1 to set a target, H to hyperspace.
  4. On arrival the station orbits the planet — find the rotating slot and align (Q deactivates audio if it bothers you while flying).

Trading basics

  • Agricultural → Industrial → back. Buy Food/Furs/Textiles on agricultural worlds, sell at industrial. Buy Computers/Machinery on the way home.
  • Stay legal at first. Narcotics, Slaves, and Firearms get you fined; only profitable once you have police-evading kit.
  • Fuel scoops pay for themselves. Skim a star (carefully — don't fly into it) for free hyperspace fuel.
  • Don't fight Thargoids unprovoked. The first one you see ends most early commanders' careers.

BBC keyboard quirks

The function keys are f0–f9 on the original BBC. In emulation they map to F1–F10 (BBC f0 → F10 isn't used in Elite). The arrow keys are also remapped to ?///</> by default — every BBC Micro player learned this layout.
