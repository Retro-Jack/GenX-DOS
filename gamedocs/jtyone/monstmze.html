Home Computer · Sinclair ZX81

3D Monster Maze

J. K. Greye Software, 1981 — Malcolm Evans. The first 3D first-person game on a home computer; an animated wireframe-style perspective maze rendered in chunky ZX81 graphics — astonishing for a 1K-base machine (16K RAM pack required).

You're dropped into a 16×18 maze with a single tyrannosaurus rex. Find the exit before Rex finds you. The screen shows what's ahead in low-res 3D perspective; status messages narrate Rex's approach ("HE IS BEHIND YOU", "REX LIES IN WAIT", "STEP IN HIS JAWS"). No sound chip on the ZX81 — the entire scare is text + the rendered shape of Rex closing in.

Getting started

  1. Tape auto-loads. Press any key at the title to start.
  2. You appear in the maze facing one direction. The screen shows the corridor in 3D perspective.
  3. Watch the status line at the bottom — it warns when Rex is near.
  4. Reach the exit (a door shape on the corridor wall) to win the level.

Controls

This emulator is keyboard-only — USB gamepads aren't supported.

KeyAction
5Turn 90° left
8Turn 90° right
7Step forward (you can't move backwards)

Status messages

MessageMeaning
"FOOTSTEPS APPROACHING"Rex has caught your scent, somewhere on the map
"REX LIES IN WAIT"He's stopped to ambush — corridor ahead is dangerous
"HE IS HUNTING FOR YOU"Active pursuit
"RUN! HE IS BEHIND YOU"Rex is in the same corridor; turn corner immediately
"STEP IN HIS JAWS"You lost

Strategy

  • Corner often. Rex can only see in straight lines. Every turn breaks his line-of-sight.
  • The maze layout is fixed per session but regenerates on each load — there's no level memorisation.
  • Sprint between turns. Movement is per-tile; chains of 7 presses move faster than the rendering catches up.
  • Sound is dead. The ZX81 has no audio. The atmosphere is all visual + text — read the bottom line constantly.
