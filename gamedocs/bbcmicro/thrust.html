Thrust

Superior Software, 1986 — Jeremy C. Smith. Gravity-physics puzzle/shooter inspired by Atari's Gravitar; one screen, deadly inertia, infinite re-tries. (Superior licensed the ports to Firebird, whose budget C64 version actually reached the shops first.)

You pilot a tiny ship through caverns to retrieve a stolen klystron pod, hooked beneath the ship by a tractor beam. The pod swings on inertia, every wall is lethal, and your only acceleration is rotational thrust. Get the pod out, escape the cavern, and refuel from depots without overheating.

Getting started

  1. Press Space at the title to start.
  2. Your ship hovers above the pod — press Space to engage the tractor beam and lift it.
  3. Thrust upward gently while rotating; the pod swings under you and bumps walls if you accelerate hard.
  4. Reach the cavern exit at the top to complete the level.

ReactorGlowing core. Shoot to destroy; counts down to explosion that wipes the cavern.

Strategy

  • Two-second bursts only. Continuous thrust both crashes you and overheats the reactor. Pulse — coast — pulse.
  • Pod swing is predictable. Like a real pendulum. Time direction reversals at the bottom of the swing for the least disturbance.
  • Don't refuel while carrying the pod. The fuel cell explodes if the pod swings into it.
  • Destroy the reactor for the bonus. Risky — you have ~5 seconds to escape the cavern after the explosion, but the score multiplier is huge.
  • Snapshot between caverns. Thrust has no in-game save, so use the save/load buttons at the bottom-left of the browser window — five slots — to bank progress before a tricky cavern.
