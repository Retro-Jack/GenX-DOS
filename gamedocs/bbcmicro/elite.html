Elite

Acornsoft, 1984 — David Braben & Ian Bell. The first open-world space trading game, with 2,048 procedurally-generated planets across 8 galaxies, fitted into 22 KB of a BBC Model B. Disc version (this build) adds the docking computer and mission content.

You start in Lave with 100 credits, a Cobra Mk III, a pulse laser, and a rating of "Harmless". Trade goods between systems, fight (or flee) the pirates that ambush you in hyperspace, dock at space stations, upgrade your ship — and over hundreds of hours of play try to reach the "Elite" combat rating with 6,400 kills.

Getting started

  1. At the title press Space. At the Load New Commander (Y/N)? prompt, press N to play the default Commander Jameson. (Y opens the in-game disc-access menu, but disc saves don't persist in this build — use the load/save buttons at the bottom-left of the browser window.)
  2. You launch from inside Lave station — match the rotation of the stars before flying out, then hit J to in-system jump to the planet.
  3. Press F7 for the market and buy Food (cheap on Lave); use F4/F5 (galactic / short-range charts) to choose a destination system, then H to hyperspace.
  4. On arrival the station orbits the planet — find the rotating slot and align (Q deactivates audio if it bothers you while flying).

Saving your progress: Elite normally saves your commander to disc, but this build's disc is read-only in the browser — an in-game Save Commander won't survive closing the tab. Use the GenX save / load buttons to keep your ship, cash, and rating between sessions. Five save slots per game — pick 1–5 from the save or load menu.

Function keys — dual purpose

Elite's function keys do one thing in flight and another at the station: F10 and F1–F3 switch cockpit views while flying, but trigger the dock actions when you're docked. (See BBC keyboard quirks below for how these map to the BBC's red f0–f3.)

Trading basics

  • Agricultural → Industrial → back. Buy Food/Furs/Textiles on agricultural worlds, sell at industrial. Buy Computers/Machinery on the way home.
  • Stay legal at first. Narcotics, Slaves, and Firearms get you fined; only profitable once you have police-evading kit.
  • Fuel scoops pay for themselves. Skim a star (carefully — don't fly into it) for free hyperspace fuel.
  • Don't fight Thargoids unprovoked. The first one you see ends most early commanders' careers.

BBC keyboard quirks

The original BBC has red function keys f0–f9. jsbeeb maps them to your PC's function-key row: f1–f9 are 1:1 (BBC f1 = F1, … f9 = F9), and BBC f0 — the front view — is F10 (the BBC has no F0 equivalent on a PC keyboard, so it sits at the end of the row). Elite doesn't use the arrow keys: flight is the classic BBC layout — </> roll, S/X pitch.
