Manic Miner

Software Projects, 1984 — Matthew Smith's original (Spectrum, 1983) ported to the Beeb by D.J. Coathupe. Twenty single-screen caverns of pixel-perfect jumping.

Willy has fallen into Surbiton's old mine. Collect every flashing key on a screen and the door opens — then walk through to the next cavern. Your oxygen ticks down constantly, and the gaps between deadly mine-life are tighter than they look.

Getting started

  1. Press any key at the title to start.
  2. Step onto a flashing key to collect it. When all keys on the screen are collected the door (right side) becomes passable.
  3. The clock at the bottom is air, not score time — run out and you suffocate.

Strategy

  • Willy's jump is fixed-arc. Distance depends entirely on how long you've been walking before take-off. Start the run early.
  • Don't reverse mid-jump. You can't — and most "impossible" jumps are people fighting the controls.
  • Eugene's Lair is the difficulty wall most first-time players hit. The trick is timing the drop onto the floor so Eugene chases you in a predictable pattern.
  • Conveyors steer your jump. Jumping with the conveyor extends range; against it shortens. Stand on conveyor, walk against it, jump opposite — long jump.
  • No save — lean on the buttons. Manic Miner has no save and no continues; use the load/save buttons at the bottom-left to snapshot progress between caverns. Five save slots per game — pick 1–5 from the save or load menu.
