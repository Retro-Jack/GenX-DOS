Exile

Superior Software, 1988 — Peter Irvin & Jeremy Smith. Open-world physics-driven exploration on a 6502; routinely cited as the BBC's technical and design high-water mark.

Your ship has crashed on the prison planet Phoebus. You explore a single huge interconnected cave system using a jet-pack with real inertia, lifting and pushing objects, solving environmental puzzles, and fighting native wildlife. There are no levels — everything happens in one continuous world, and every object obeys gravity, wind, and recoil.

Getting started

  1. Press Space at the title to start.
  2. You begin at the crash site. Walk left or right with Q/W and try the jet-pack with P (up) / L (down).
  3. Fire your gun with Space — and almost everything in the world can be picked up, thrown, or used.

Environmental physics

  • Wind currents push you sideways in some chambers — you can ride them up vertical shafts.
  • Water drowns you; you carry a finite air supply, swap for the diving suit found mid-game.
  • Heat / radiation damages over time; the protective suit blocks specific zones.
  • Recoil from fired weapons pushes you in flight — useful as emergency thrust.
  • Object inertia persists: thrown grenades arc realistically, dropped keys fall.

Strategy

  • Map obsessively. Exile has no in-game map and many one-way drops. Sketch room exits as you go.
  • Carry the right item. Some doors need specific keys; some creatures only die to specific weapons; the jet-pack drains energy faster with heavy items.
  • Use creatures against each other. Native birds attack guards, robots fight wildlife. Lead enemies to enemies.
  • Save often. Exile is brutal to first-time explorers, and its own "shutdown" save writes to the disc — which is read-only in the browser, so it won't persist. Use the load/save buttons at the bottom-left instead: five save slots per game, pick 1–5 from the save or load menu.
