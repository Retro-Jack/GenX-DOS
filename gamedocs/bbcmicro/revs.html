Revs

Acornsoft, 1985 — Geoff Crammond. A serious Formula Three simulation and the direct ancestor of Crammond's later Formula One Grand Prix series. One circuit: a full 3D Silverstone with real undulations, a manual gearbox and adjustable aerodynamics.

This isn't an arcade racer — it's a simulation. The car has a real clutch-and-gearbox feel: you start in neutral with the engine off, and nothing happens until you fire it up and select a gear. The track is genuinely three-dimensional, with crests and dips that hide the road ahead, and the handling rewards smoothness over bravado. You qualify for grid position, then race the AI cars around Silverstone.

Getting started

  1. The disc autoboots to an instructions screen; press Space to continue. Pick Practice (option 1) to learn the circuit — the game suggests setting the rear wings to 40 and the front to 32 to start.
  2. Start the engine with T. To pull away from a standstill, hold T, S and Q together — that holds the clutch in — then release to let it bite. You begin in neutral, so nothing happens until the engine's running and you're in gear.
  3. Steer with L (left) and + (right — that's the =/+ key on a PC), and hold Space to amplify the steering through tighter corners. The game also reads an analogue joystick, which this emulator maps to mouse motion if you prefer proportional steering.

Controls

These are the keys from the game's own instructions screen. Steer-right is the BBC + key — on a PC that's the =/+ key (this emulator also maps ; to the same BBC key). "Return to pits" is the BBC red f0 key, which this emulator maps to F10.

Strategy

  • Master the standing start. Engine off means no movement. Press T to start it, then hold T+S+Q to slip the clutch off the line without stalling.
  • Learn the Space steering-boost. Held down it amplifies your steering input — essential for the tighter corners, and the single most important thing to master on the keyboard.
  • Try the analogue option. Crammond built Revs around an analogue joystick and said it's much easier that way; this emulator maps that to the mouse, giving proportional steering the keys can't.
  • Smooth, not fast. Brake in a straight line before the corner, get the gear right on entry, feed the power in on exit. Stabbing the throttle mid-corner spins you.
  • Qualify, then race. A lap or two of practice to read Silverstone's blind crests pays off far more than raw speed — you can't see over some of the rises until you're on them.
