River Raid

Activision, 1982 — designed by Carol Shaw (the first woman to design a major commercial video game). Procedurally-generated infinite vertical shoot-em-up with fuel-management.

A jet flies up an endless river bombing enemy ships, helicopters, planes, balloons, and bridges. The river narrows into chokepoints; fly through fuel depots to refuel. Hit anything (riverbank, enemy, sky) — you lose a life. Each bridge cleared advances a "level" (the river ahead gets harder).

Getting started

  1. Press V (Select) to pick game variant.
  2. Press Enter (Reset) to start.
  3. Steer the jet left/right; ↑ accelerates, ↓ slows; Space fires.

Strategy

  • Fuel discipline. Fly THROUGH depots, don't shoot them — touching a depot refuels you, shooting it scores 80 pts but no fuel. Mix as needed.
  • Choke-points. Where the river narrows to one-jet-wide, slow with ↓ and don't fire — a missed bullet's pixel-cost-per-frame is your turning radius.
  • Bridges = level boundary. Always destroyable, worth 500 pts, and they refill a sliver of fuel on some variants. Centre the bridge before bombing.
  • Don't accelerate when in trouble. Speed-up is a multiplier on score per second, but reaction window shrinks. Pace yourself in dense lanes.
  • Bratislava cap. The score counter is 32-bit (8 hex digits) — effectively infinite for human players. Activision sent patches to anyone scoring 15K+.

Atari 2600 controls

Single-stick + fire. The 2600 stick's 4-way digital nature limits diagonal precision — pre-align rather than mid-flight correct.
