Console · Atari 2600

Yars' Revenge

Atari, 1982 — designed by Howard Scott Warshaw. Top-selling original 2600 title; based on an aborted Cinematronics arcade port (Star Castle). The "neutral zone" buzzing pattern is the cart ROM's TIA garbage repurposed as art.

A Yar (insectoid) faces the Qotile, an enemy fortress on the right side of the screen behind a destructible shield. Nibble the shield from one side, fly through to touch the Qotile's core, return to the left side, then aim the Zorlon Cannon (which fires whenever you're carrying its charge) at the exposed core. The Qotile periodically launches the "Swirl" — a one-hit-kill projectile.

Getting started

  1. Press V (Select) to cycle game variant.
  2. Press Enter (Reset) to start.
  3. Move with the joystick; Space fires.

How a round flows

  1. Eat or shoot pieces of the Qotile's shield to weaken it.
  2. Touch the Qotile's core (no shield in the way) — you now carry the Zorlon Cannon charge.
  3. Fly back left of the playfield's mid-line to position the cannon.
  4. Press Space to fire the cannon. A horizontal beam shoots right — only it can destroy the Qotile.
  5. Dodge the Swirl (homing fireball) the whole time. The Neutral Zone (centre band) shelters you from Qotile gunfire but doesn't stop the Swirl.

Strategy

  • Eat shields fast. Touching shield blocks consumes them and gives you the Zorlon charge. Faster than shooting them apart.
  • Neutral Zone refuge. The buzzing centre band protects you from Qotile bullets but is harmless ground. Park there between attacks.
  • Watch for the Swirl wind-up. The Qotile pulses before transforming into the Swirl. As soon as it strobes, prepare to dodge — the Swirl tracks you.
  • Cannon aim. The Zorlon fires from YOUR vertical position straight right. Align your Y with the Qotile before pressing fire.
  • Self-damage. Your own cannon beam can hit you on the way to the Qotile! Get out of its horizontal lane immediately after firing.

Atari 2600 controls

Single-stick + fire. Enter (Reset) starts the game. The buzz-pattern in the centre is the contents of the cartridge ROM rendered as TIA noise — purely cosmetic, but it's why Yars' looks alive even at rest.
