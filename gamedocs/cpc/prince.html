Prince of Persia

Broderbund, 1990 — Jordan Mechner. The rotoscoped platformer that made every other 8-bit hero look stiff: sixty minutes to cross the Sultan's dungeons, outfight his guards, and reach the princess before Jaffar does.

On the Grand Vizier Jaffar's orders you are arrested, stripped of your sword and thrown into the palace dungeons. The princess has been given an hour to marry him or die — and that hour is the game's clock, running whether you are climbing, fighting or standing still. The Prince moves with real weight: he runs up to a jump, grabs ledges as he falls, and a mistimed step onto a loose slab is as deadly as a guard's blade.

Getting started

  1. Click the game area to enable sound and controls.
  2. At the title screen, press a joystick button or any key to start.
  3. You begin unarmed — find your sword before you meet a guard who has one.

Controls are transcribed from the Broderbund manual's Amstrad CPC pages. This is a joystick game first — the keyboard scheme below is the manual's stated stand-in for it.

Saving: the game's own Ctrl+G writes to disc and Ctrl+L reloads it at the title screen — but the disc image here is read-only, so use the save / load buttons in the bottom-left corner instead.

Strategy

  • The clock never stops. Sixty minutes covers the whole game, not each level — dithering on level two costs you the ending.
  • Run up to the long jumps. A standing jump clears very little; take three steps first and commit.
  • Fall deliberately. Holding the button as you drop makes the Prince grab the ledge below, turning a fatal fall into a controlled climb-down.
  • Step, don't stride, near edges and slabs. A gentle push walks him carefully — the difference between triggering a loose slab and easing past it.
  • Back off mid-duel. Retreating is a real move: pull away, let a guard over-commit, then step in.
  • Practice on the early levels. Shift+L exists precisely so you can rehearse the jumps and swordplay without burning the real hour.
