Elite

Firebird, 1985–1986 — David Braben & Ian Bell. The open-universe space-trader template that every later game in the genre rebuilds from. Eight galaxies, 256 planets each, all procedurally generated.

Start with a Cobra Mk III, 100 credits, and the rank "Harmless". Buy low, sell high, fight off pirates, dock at stations, work up through the combat ratings. Reaching Elite is a long-haul commitment — most players measure it in weeks of real time.

Getting started

  1. Game auto-boots — bundle pre-types run"elite.
  2. Click the game area to enable sound and controls.
  3. Press 1 to launch from the station when you're ready to fly.

Saving: on a real CPC, @ while docked saves your commander to disc — here the disc image is read-only, so use the save/load buttons at the bottom-left of the browser window instead: they snapshot the whole machine and keep it across sessions, with five slots in the save and load menus. Dock first, then save — mid-dogfight snapshots preserve the dogfight.

Strategy

  • Trade routes first. Industrial systems buy food and minerals; agricultural systems buy machinery and computers. Two-leg run, fixed profit.
  • Buy a docking computer early. Manual docking is the most unforgiving skill in the game — the auto-docker is worth every credit.
  • Don't sell the slave trade. The Galactic Police remember; you'll be marked Fugitive across the system.
  • Asteroid mining is steady low-risk income — fit a mining laser, blast the asteroids you run across in flight, and scoop the splinters they break into.
