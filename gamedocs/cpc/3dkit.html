3D Construction Kit

Domark / Incentive, 1991 — Ian Andrew & the Freescape team. The publicly-released version of the Freescape engine that powered Driller and Total Eclipse, packaged as a level editor anyone could use.

Two modes — Editor (build solid-3D worlds with cubes, prisms, pyramids, sloped roofs) and Walk (move through the world you built). Bundled with the FCG demo world. The Freescape engine renders flat-shaded polygons at single-digit frames per second on the CPC — by 1991 standards, miraculous; by today's, atmospheric.

Getting started

  1. Disc autoboots — bundle pre-types run"disc.
  2. Click the canvas first to give it keyboard focus.
  3. Choose Editor or Walk from the start menu.

In-game S/L save and load to the disc, but the disc is read-only in the browser — your world is lost when you close the tab. Use the load/save buttons at the bottom-left to keep a build across sessions. Five save slots per game — pick 1–5 from the save or load menu.

Tips

  • Start with the bundled FCG world. Walk it first, then open it in the Editor to see how the pieces fit before building from scratch.
  • Polygon budget is real. Every flat face is rendered each frame — keep object counts low or the framerate collapses.
  • Conditions are the puzzle layer. Each object can have an IF/THEN script (touch trigger, shoot trigger, timer). The bundle's manual covers the FCL syntax — without that the Editor is purely visual.
  • 3D Construction Kit II followed in 1992 with curved surfaces; the original is the period-correct CPC entry.
