Robotron: 2084

Atari, 1987 (port of the 1982 Williams arcade by Eugene Jarvis & Larry DeMar). The twin-stick shooter blueprint: one stick moves, the other aims/fires.

The last human family. Robots have killed everyone else. You're trapped in a square arena with waves of robots, hulks, brains, electrodes, and grunts. Save the Mommy/Daddy/Mikey humans for bonus points; shoot everything else. Save the family from the brains (who mutate them into deadly prog enemies) while staying alive.

Getting started

  1. Press Enter to start.
  2. Move with the arrows; fire direction is controlled separately.
  3. 7800 port note: the ProLine is a single stick with two fire buttons (Z/X), so the 7800 port has no native twin-stick aiming. For a true twin-stick feel, plug in a second gamepad (auto-assigned to Player 2).

Humans

Mikey (boy), Mommy, Daddy. Touch them to rescue → 1000, 2000, 3000, 4000, 5000 pts (increases per rescue in the same wave). Hulks and Brains will kill them if you don't reach them first.

Strategy

  • Save humans first. Each saved family member multiplies your score. Race the Hulks to humans on every wave.
  • Ignore Hulks. They cannot be killed. Just keep distance and shoot AROUND them to push them back.
  • Prioritise Brains. The longer a Brain lives, the more humans it converts. Kill them fast even before clearing Grunts.
  • Electrode camping. Electrodes spawn in random spots each wave. Memorise the dangerous corners.
  • Constantly move. Standing still = death from any direction. The game's pace is so high that the moment your thumb relaxes, you die.

Atari 7800 controls

Robotron is fundamentally a twin-stick game, and the 7800 ProLine is a single stick with two fire buttons — so the 7800 port is a compromise: you move with the D-pad and fire with Z/X. For something closer to the arcade's two-stick feel, plug in a second gamepad (auto-assigned to Player 2) and use one for movement, one for firing.
