Elite

Acornsoft, 1984 — David Braben & Ian Bell. The Acorn Electron conversion, out within weeks of the BBC original: the same 2,048-planet, 8-galaxy universe squeezed onto the Electron's more limited hardware.

You start in Lave with 100 credits, a Cobra Mk III, a pulse laser, and a rating of "Harmless". Trade goods between systems, fight (or flee) the pirates that ambush you, dock at space stations, upgrade your ship. The Electron's hardware forced some cuts — no split-screen view, no suns, no Thargoids, fewer ship types and a noticeably slower frame rate (which arguably makes it easier) — but the trading-and-combat heart of Elite is intact.

Getting started

  1. At the title, press Space, then N at the Load New Commander (Y/N)? prompt to fly the default Commander Jameson.
  2. You launch from inside Lave station — match the rotation of the stars before flying out, then hit J to in-system jump.
  3. Press Alt+8 for the market (buy Food on Lave); Alt+5/Alt+6 bring up the charts to choose a destination, then H to hyperspace.
  4. On arrival the station orbits the planet — line up with the rotating slot to dock.

Saving: on a real Electron, Elite saves your commander to tape — but this bundle runs from a fixed, read-only snapshot with no writable storage (and this emulator has no save-state function that can be implemented here), so progress can't be kept between sessions. Every visit starts fresh as Commander Jameson; the startup Y (load commander) option has nothing to load.

Function keys — dual purpose

The Electron has no function-key row — its red function keys are FUNC + a number, and this emulator maps FUNC to Alt. In flight these switch cockpit views; when docked they trigger the station actions.

Trading basics

  • Agricultural → Industrial → back. Food/Furs/Textiles on agricultural worlds, sell at industrial; Computers/Machinery on the return.
  • Stay legal at first. Narcotics, Slaves, and Firearms get you fined; profitable only with police-evading kit.
  • Fuel scoop is cargo-only here. The Electron has no suns, so you can't skim a star for fuel — the scoop only picks up cargo canisters left by destroyed ships.
  • Slower pace, kinder dogfights. The Electron runs Elite noticeably slower than the BBC, giving you more reaction time when pirates jump you.

Electron vs BBC version

The universe is the same — 2,048 planets, 8 galaxies, the trading-and-combat loop — but the Electron's hardware forced cuts. With no 6845 CRTC the BBC's memory-saving split-screen mode couldn't be reprogrammed, so the dashboard/space split, the suns and the Thargoids were all dropped, along with some ship types, and the whole game runs noticeably slower (which makes it a touch easier).
