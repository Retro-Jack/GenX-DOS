Starship Command

Acornsoft, 1983 — Peter Irvin's first commercial release (later co-author of Exile). Rotating-starfield space combat with active power-budget management; a quiet design landmark.

You command a single starship defending a sector against escalating waves of alien fighters. The starfield rotates around you (not the ship around the field) — you point a fixed-position cross-hair and the universe spins underneath. Energy is finite: thrusting, firing torpedoes and raising shields all draw on it, and shields and scanners share one circuit so you run one or the other. Balancing it all is the whole game.

Getting started

  1. Press Space at the title to start.
  2. You're in the middle of the screen; aliens jump in from the edges. Rotate the universe with Z/X until your nose lines up.
  3. Fire torpedoes with N and thrust with M — both draw energy, so don't hold them needlessly.
  4. Press B for shields when under fire; shields switch your scanners off, so flip back to scanners with V when it's clear (or let C auto-changeover juggle them).

Flight controls

This game supports a USB gamepad — left/right on the D-pad or stick rotates, up thrusts, down brakes, and any face button fires torpedoes. The command-console keys stay on the keyboard.

The energy budget

  • Thrust / brake (M / ,) — manoeuvring costs energy.
  • Torpedoes (N) — every shot is a drain.
  • Shields (B) — absorb hits, but raising them switches scanners off.
  • Scanners (V) — long-range tracker so you can pre-aim where the next wave appears; shares the circuit with shields.
  • Dampers (2/Q rotation, 3/W velocity) — steady your handling; switch them off for sharper, twitchier control.
  • Sustained combat empties the energy fast — pick your moments.

Strategy

  • Don't spray torpedoes. Fire N only with a target lined up — each shot costs energy.
  • Shields vs scanners. B raises shields but blinds your scanners; V flips back. Let auto-changeover (C) handle the swap once the screen gets busy.
  • Escape capsules are the last resort. F/G eject you (port/starboard) from a doomed ship — you live to fight on, but the starship's gone.
  • Predict the rotation. The cross-hair never moves; you're flying the cosmos around it. Lead your rotation slightly past the target to compensate for delay.
