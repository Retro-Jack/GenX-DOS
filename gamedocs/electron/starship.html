Starship Command

Acornsoft, 1983 — Peter Irvin's first commercial release (later co-author of Exile). Rotating-starfield space combat with active power-budget management; a quiet design landmark.

You command a single starship defending a sector against escalating waves of alien fighters. The starfield rotates around you (not the ship around the field) — you point a fixed-position cross-hair and the universe spins underneath. Your power budget is a single bar split between Engines, Lasers, Shields, and Scanners; balancing it is the whole game.

Getting started

  1. Press Space at the title to start.
  2. You're in the middle of the screen; aliens jump in from the edges. Rotate the universe with Z/X until your nose lines up.
  3. Hold Return to fire lasers. Lasers drain Power; running it dry leaves you defenceless for several seconds.
  4. Press S to bring up the shields, but shields also drain Power — only raise them under fire.

The power budget

  • Engines — thrust + rotation responsiveness. Bleeds while moving.
  • Lasers — every shot is a drain.
  • Shields — absorb hits but drain continuously while raised.
  • Scanners — passive long-range tracker so you can pre-aim where the next wave appears.
  • Power regenerates between waves but not during. Sustained combat empties the bar fast.

Strategy

  • Lasers off when not firing. Hold Return only when an alien is in your sights — don't spray.
  • Shields up at impact only. Raise the instant a laser bolt is incoming, drop the moment it's clear.
  • Hyperjump = retreat with honour. Survives a no-win round but costs the sector. Use sparingly.
  • Predict the rotation. The cross-hair never moves; you're flying the cosmos around it. Lead your rotation slightly past the target to compensate for delay.
