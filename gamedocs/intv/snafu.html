Console · Mattel Intellivision

Snafu

Mattel, 1981 — designed by Eric Wels. Up to four players. The Intellivision's take on the Blockade / Tron-lightcycle genre.

You and up to three opponents trail a continuously-growing wall behind your moving cursor. Crash into another player's wall, your own tail, or the screen edge and you lose. Wraparound between rounds resets the arena. Two-player co-op against AI is the headline mode; four-player with multitap-style controllers is the marquee feature.

Getting started

  1. Pick a game variant on the keypad — 1- to 4-player, with AI or human opponents.
  2. Each player's controller drives their own snake. The snake never stops moving; you only steer it.
  3. Last snake alive wins the round; first to a target score wins the match.

Strategy

  • Build space, then attack. A wall you've built between you and an opponent is valuable territory — don't waste it.
  • Don't paint yourself into a corner. The classic mistake — locking yourself in a maze you can't escape.
  • Drive opponents into walls. Aggressive cuts that force an opponent to choose between your wall and their own usually kill them.
  • Save the boost. Speed bursts are a one-shot reaction tool — useful for darting past a closing gap, not for sustained moves.

Intellivision controls

Despite the 16-direction disc, Snafu only reads the four cardinal directions. The diagonals don't matter; you can rest a finger on the disc rim and just push N/S/E/W. Both players use the disc; the action buttons may differ by game variant.
