Console · Mattel Intellivision

Astrosmash

Mattel, 1981 — John Sohl. Mattel's best-selling Intellivision title; ~1 million carts.

A vertical shooter with weather-physics rocks: fragments of varying sizes tumble from the top of the screen, splitting into smaller pieces when hit. UFOs cross periodically, firing back. The score wraps and the difficulty ramps automatically — each game ends with all your reserves spent. Astrosmash was the Asteroids/Space Invaders hybrid Mattel hadn't licensed.

Getting started

  1. Your laser cannon sits at the bottom of the screen — disc moves it left/right.
  2. Three lives. Touching a rock or being shot ends a life.
  3. The Hyperspace button warps you to a random screen position if cornered.

Strategy

  • Don't shoot the small fragments unless they're aimed at you. Splitting a big rock unnecessarily creates more incoming.
  • Hyperspace is a last resort. The new position is random — could be safer or worse. Save it for when you'd otherwise die.
  • Speed-difficulty climbs automatically. Conserve reflexes for later screens — easy runs early get sloppy and you forget the rock physics later.
  • UFO bullets are the killer. The rocks announce themselves on the radar; UFO shots come straight in. Watch the upper screen.

Intellivision controls

The lower side button fires; the upper side buttons trigger hyperspace. The disc is 16-direction but only the left/right components matter here. Hold the fire button for rapid fire.
