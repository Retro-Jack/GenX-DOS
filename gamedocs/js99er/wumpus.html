Hunt the Wumpus

Texas Instruments (Gregory Yob, ported by TI), 1980 — Solid State Software cart. The graphical TI-99/4A re-interpretation of Yob's 1973 mainframe BASIC cave-deduction classic. The cave map is drawn on-screen so you don't have to keep one on paper.

Twenty caves arranged as a dodecahedron, each connected to three others. Somewhere is the Wumpus. Adjacent rooms may have superbats (random teleport) or bottomless pits (instant death). Sensory hints — "I smell a Wumpus", "I feel a draft", "I hear bats" — tell you something's adjacent but never which tunnel. Deduce the Wumpus's location and shoot your crooked arrow.

Getting started

  1. Cart auto-boots to the TI title screen; press 2 for "FOR HUNT THE WUMPUS PRESS 2".
  2. Choose Wumpus 1 (easier — small cave) or Wumpus 2 (full cave).

Strategy

  • Map by tunnel triplets. Each room shows three connecting room numbers. Pencil them as you visit; you can pre-compute danger zones from sensed warnings.
  • Triangulate the Wumpus. Two rooms both reporting "I smell a Wumpus" share exactly one neighbour — that's the Wumpus's chamber.
  • Bats reverse luck. A superbat encounter teleports you to a random room (often into a worse one); survivable but no info gained.
  • Pits are forever. "I feel a draft" + a single unknown neighbour = don't go there.
  • One arrow. A missed shot wakes the Wumpus, who relocates to an adjacent room and may move into yours on his next move — game over.
