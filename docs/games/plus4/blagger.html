Blagger

Alligata, 1984 — Antony Crowther's hop-and-grab single-screen platformer ported from his earlier C64 hit. Roger the Dodger is a master cat-burglar; nick all the gold keys on each screen, then leg it to the exit before the time-bonus drains.

System help — the Commodore Plus/4 controls

Twenty single-screen heists. Each screen has a few floating gold keys you must touch to collect; once you've grabbed them all, a door or floor section opens and you can fall/run to the next screen. Patrolling guards and animated hazards (firemen, drifting cooks, swooping bats) kill on contact. One life per touch; three lives total.

Getting started

  1. The .prg auto-loads; click the game area to enable sound and controls.
  2. Press F1 at the title to start (or wait for the attract demo).

Strategy

  • Plan the route before grabbing the last key. The exit only opens after the final key — you don't want to be on the wrong platform when the route home changes.
  • Jumps go where you're already heading. Crowther's engine commits to your horizontal velocity at take-off; you can't change direction mid-air.
  • Patrolling enemies have fixed patterns. Watch one full cycle before stepping out — most screens have a 2-second safe window between passes.
  • Falls don't kill, but missing the platform does. Roger survives any drop height; the danger is landing on a guard rather than the floor.
