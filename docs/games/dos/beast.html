Personal Computer · IBM PC

Beast

1984 — Dan Baker, Alan Brown, Mark Hamilton and Derrick Shadel. Crush the beasts between two blocks. They are faster than you and there are more of them every level.

System help — the IBM PC controls

A text-mode puzzle played entirely in characters, and one of the few games on this shelf where stopping to think is the whole point. You push blocks around a field; a beast squashed between a block you are pushing and something solid behind it dies. Everything else about the game — the speed, the numbers, the way they herd you — exists to stop you having the time to work it out.

Getting started

  1. The title screen shows the status line along the bottom: beasts remaining, level, time, lives and score.
  2. Press Space to play.
  3. Push a block into a beast with something solid behind it. That is the entire method.

Controls

From the game's own status line and title screen.

KeyboardAction
↑ ↓ ← →Move, and push the block you move into
Numeric keypadThe same, including the diagonals
SpaceStart playing
A letter keyChange level, from the title screen
F1High scores
F2Speed
F10Quit

The lighter blocks can be pushed. The static ones cannot be pushed or pulled, and are what you squash things against.

Tips

  • Work at the edges. The outer wall is the most reliable thing to crush against, and it cannot be moved out from under you.
  • A block you cannot reach behind is useless. Before you commit to a push, check there is something solid on the far side of the beast.
  • They come to you. This is exploitable: set the block up first and let the beast walk into the gap.
  • Later levels change the beasts, not the rule. The method never stops working; the room to use it is what disappears.
