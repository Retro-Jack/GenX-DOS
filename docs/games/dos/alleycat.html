Alley Cat

1984 — Bill Williams, published by IBM. A cat in a back alley, a row of windows above it, and a female cat somewhere inside who is the entire reason for any of this.

System help — the IBM PC controls

Freddy the cat jumps from dustbins to washing lines to windowsills, and through any open window into somebody's flat — where a different small game is waiting: a fish tank, a mouse hole, a shelf of plant pots, a dog. Clear enough of them and you reach the cat you came for. It is strange, generous, slightly melancholy, and looks like nothing else on the machine.

Getting started

  1. You start in the alley. The dustbins and the washing line are the way up.
  2. Windows open and close. Jump through an open one to enter a room.
  3. Each room is its own small game with its own goal. Finish it and you are back in the alley, one heart nearer.

Controls

From the manual. Movement is the cursor keys; a digital joystick works the same way, and there is no analogue support — the game came from a machine that had never heard of it.

KeyboardAction
← →Walk
↑Jump
↑ with ← or →Jump in that direction
↓Drop down from a line, fence or ledge
Hold a directionRun — a running jump goes much further than a standing one

The distance of a jump depends on how fast you were already moving. Almost everything that looks impossible is a running jump you took from a standstill.

Tips

  • Get moving before you leave the ground. This is the whole skill of the alley.
  • Watch the windows before you climb. They open and shut on their own schedule; arriving at a shut one wastes the climb.
  • Each room has its own trick. The fish tank, the mice, the plants and the dog all want something different, and none of them explains itself.
  • It is a gentle game with a cruel edge. Falling costs you, and the alley is mostly things to fall off.
