Paratrooper

1982 — Greg Kuperberg, published by Orion Software. A gun at the bottom of the screen, helicopters above it, and men coming down on parachutes. Let four of them reach the ground on one side and they will blow the gun up from underneath you.

System help — the IBM PC controls

One of the first games anyone bought for an IBM PC, and for a while one of the only ones. Your gun turret sits at the bottom centre and swings through an arc; helicopters cross the sky dropping paratroopers, and later the jets arrive, whose pilots do not miss. It is a fixed shooter of the purest kind — nothing to collect, nowhere to go, just the arc of the gun and the arithmetic of how many are already on the ground.

Getting started

  1. The title screen offers instructions on I — worth a look, they are the game's own.
  2. Press Space to begin.
  3. Ignore the joystick offer. The title screen names a joystick button beside the space bar. This emulator has no way to pass a joystick or gamepad through to a DOS game, so that road leads nowhere — it is the keyboard or nothing here.
  4. The gun starts still. It only moves once you tell it to, and it keeps moving until you stop it.

Controls

Taken from the game's own instructions screen. The gun is steered from the numeric keypad, and the unusual part is that firing and stopping are the same key.

KeyboardAction
← or 4Swing the gun anticlockwise — it keeps going
→ or 6Swing the gun clockwise — it keeps going
↑ or 8Stop the gun where it is, and fire
IInstructions, from the title screen
SpaceStart a keyboard game, from the title screen

Because ↑ both halts the swing and fires, aiming is a matter of starting the gun moving and stopping it at the right moment. You do not line up and then shoot; stopping is shooting.

Scoring

The game's own table:

TargetScore
Helicopter or jet10 points
Paratrooper5 points
Bomb30 points
Each bullet firedcosts you 1 point

Tips

  • Count the ones on the ground. Four on a single side is the end of you. Three is an emergency and you should be clearing that side rather than scoring.
  • Shoot the parachutes, not the men. A trooper hit while still falling is worth the same and cannot land.
  • Bullets cost a point each. Spraying the sky is how a promising game ends with a mediocre score.
  • The jets are a different game. Once you survive the first round of helicopters they arrive, and the manual's own warning is worth repeating: every jet pilot has a deadly aim.
