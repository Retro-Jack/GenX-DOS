Kingdom of Kroz

1987 — Scott Miller, Apogee Software. Apogee's first game, and the one where episodic shareware was invented. Search the caverns for the Magical Amulet, with a whip and a dwindling supply of gems.

System help — the IBM PC controls

A dungeon drawn entirely in the PC's own character set, and the game that paid for everything Apogee did afterwards — Miller split a seventy-five level game into episodes, gave the first away, and charged for the rest. The model went on to carry Commander Keen, which sits further along this same shelf. Gems are your life: every monster that touches you takes some, and when they run out the next one kills you.

Getting started

  1. It asks whether you have a colour or monochrome display. Press C for colour.
  2. The title screen asks whether you are a Novice, Experienced or Advanced player. This sets the difficulty.
  3. You are the smiling face. The first time you touch any new object, the game stops and tells you what it is — which is the only manual you get.

Controls

From the game's own title screen. The diagonals are not optional: most levels cannot be finished without them.

What you will find

  • Gems are your life force. Each monster that reaches you costs one or more; run out and the next one is fatal.
  • Whips are ammunition, lying about the levels. They kill everything adjacent and knock down weak walls.
  • Teleport spells move you somewhere else on the level.
  • Slow-time spells let you move faster than the monsters for a while.
  • Invisibility makes you invisible to yourself while the monsters see you exactly as well as before. It is worth a great many points, which is the only reason anyone takes it.
  • Staircases go down. Some are locked, and the key is somewhere on the level.

Tips

  • Whip before you are surrounded. It clears every adjacent square at once, so the moment to use it is when several things are closing, not after one has landed.
  • Weak walls are routes. The whip breaks them, which is often the way past something you cannot fight.
  • Diagonals are the difference between escaping a pincer and being caught by it.
  • Touch new things deliberately. The explanation costs you nothing the first time and everything if you guess instead.
