The Adventures of Captain Comic

1988 — Michael A. Denio. Find three treasures on the planet Tambi and get off it again. The shareware platformer that showed the PC could do this at all.

System help — the IBM PC controls

Comic arrives on Tambi to recover three stolen treasures, and the planet is a connected world rather than a series of levels — you wander it, find the things that let you reach the places you could not, and go back. The door key, the wand, the boots and the shield all open up parts of the map. Written by one person and given away, which in 1988 on a PC was close to unheard of.

Getting started

  1. A text page appears as the game loads. Press K here to define your own keys, J for a joystick, Esc to leave, or any other key to carry on.
  2. When loading finishes, press a key for the introduction screens; the game starts after them.
  3. You begin with five lives and nothing in your inventory.
  4. You cannot fire until you have found at least one can of Blastola Cola.

Controls

From COMIC.DOC, which ships with the game. The unusual choices — Insert to fire, Caps Lock to teleport — are the game's own.

KeyboardAction
←Pressed briefly, turn to face left; when already facing left, walk left
→The same, to the right
SpaceJump — the longer you hold it, the higher. You can steer a little in mid-air
InsertFire, once you have Blastola Cola
AltOpen a door, once you have the door key
Caps LockTeleport, once you have the wand
EscPause, or quit
F1 / F2Sound on and off

Each can of Blastola Cola lets one more shot be in the air at once; with all five, the stream reaches right across the screen. Holding fire drains your weapon energy, which recharges when you let go.

Tips

  • Two energies, not one. Weapon energy recharges by itself when you stop firing; shield energy is what keeps you alive.
  • The turn is a separate action from the walk. A brief tap turns Comic; that is deliberate, and it stops you walking off ledges while aiming.
  • Every item opens up the map. When you are stuck, the answer is usually somewhere you have already been, reachable now that you have the boots.
  • Jump height is how long you hold the key, so short hops are as deliberate as long ones.
