M.U.L.E.

Ozark Softscape / Electronic Arts, 1983 — Dani Bunten Berry. Four colonists on the planet Irata compete to amass the most wealth across 6 or 12 turns by farming, mining, and trading. Among the most-imitated multi-player economic games ever made.

Each colonist claims land plots, outfits them with M.U.L.E.s (Multiple Use Labor Elements) configured for Food / Energy / Smithore / Crystite production, then trades surpluses on an open market. Auctions, sabotage, and the colony's collective survival weave into the win condition.

Getting started

  1. Press F4 Start at the title. Choose number of players (1–4) and race (Mechtron, Bonzoid, Flapper, Spheroid, Leggite, Packer, Humanoid, Gollumer).
  2. Each turn:
    1. Land selection — claim a free plot or pass.
    2. Development — buy a M.U.L.E. at the Pub/Store, outfit it, install it on a plot.
    3. Production / Random event.
    4. Auction — buy or sell resources on the trading floor.
  3. After turn 6 (short) or 12 (long), highest total score wins.

Strategy

  • Food first turn 1. Running out of food shortens your next turn — even the leader cripples themselves.
  • Energy second. A plot without energy produces nothing. Two energy plots usually covers a small empire.
  • Mountain crystite is the winning move. Crystite has no in-colony use — every unit you mine becomes pure off-world income.
  • The colony must survive. If colony food/energy collapses, every player loses points. A last-place player should still sell at fair prices.
  • Buy low, hoard, then sell at the colony crunch. When a shortage event hits, prices spike — that's the moment to dump stock.
  • The Pub / random events. Visiting the pub on the way to install a M.U.L.E. can drain your time bar — but pays in lucky cash. Risk vs. setup.

Atari 800XL controls

Joystick per player; up to 4 simultaneous players if 4 joysticks are connected. The auction phase puts all colonists on screen simultaneously in real-time bidding — chaotic and central to the game's reputation.
