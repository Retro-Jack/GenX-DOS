ELIZA

A Model 100 BASIC rendering of ELIZA — Joseph Weizenbaum's 1966 "Rogerian psychotherapist", one of the first programs ever to hold a conversation in natural language.

You talk; ELIZA listens — and turns your own words back on you as gentle questions, the way a therapist might. It understands nothing, of course, but the trick still lands sixty years on, and there's something perfect about it running on a machine you could once have slipped in a briefcase.

Getting started

  1. ELIZA loads and greets you: "HI. I'M ELIZA. WHAT'S YOUR PROBLEM?"
  2. Type a sentence — a worry, a thought, anything — and press ENTER.
  3. ELIZA reflects it back and asks you to go on. Keep the conversation running as long as you like.

Controls

There are no game keys — you just talk to it in plain English:

KeyAction
Type & ENTERSay something to ELIZA and get its reply
Type SHUT UPEnd the session

A little history

Weizenbaum wrote the original ELIZA at MIT to show how shallow machine "understanding" really was — and was famously unsettled when people bared their souls to it anyway. The psychotherapist script (properly called DOCTOR) became the best-known, and type-in versions like this one spread across every home computer of the era, the Model 100 included.
