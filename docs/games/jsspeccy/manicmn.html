Manic Miner

Bug-Byte, 1983 — Matthew Smith's original. Twenty single-screen caverns; one of the most-ported games of the 8-bit era and arguably the Speccy platformer.

Willy has fallen into Surbiton's old mine. Collect every flashing key on a screen and the door opens. Your air ticks down constantly, the gaps between deadly mine-life are tighter than they look, and the first few caverns lull you before the difficulty wall hits.

Getting started

  1. Tape auto-loads. Press any key at the title.
  2. Step onto a flashing key to collect it; when all keys are collected the door on the right opens.
  3. The bottom-bar timer is AIR, not score — run out and you suffocate.

Controls

This game supports a USB gamepad — left/right on the D-pad or stick moves Willy, and any face button jumps.

Caverns 1-10

  1. Central Cavern — tutorial
  2. The Cold Room — penguin patrols
  3. The Menagerie — moving platforms
  4. Abandoned Uranium Workings — falling platforms
  5. Eugene's Lair — the first real difficulty wall
  6. Processing Plant
  7. The Vat
  8. Miner Willy meets the Kong Beast
  9. Wacky Amoebatrons
  10. The Endorian Forest

Caverns 11-20 include the Solar Power Generator and the legendary Final Barrier — most players never see them without cheating.

Strategy

  • Fixed-arc jumps. Distance depends entirely on running speed at takeoff. Start the run early.
  • No mid-air reversal. Most "impossible" jumps are people fighting the controls.
  • Eugene's Lair is the wall — the trick is timing the drop onto the floor so Eugene chases in a predictable pattern.
  • Conveyors steer your jump. With the conveyor extends range; against shortens. Stand on conveyor, walk against, jump opposite = long jump.
