Defender

Williams, 1980 — Eugene Jarvis's side-scroller, and a byword for a complicated control panel.

System help — the arcade controls

Defend ten humanoids on a scrolling planet surface from landers that carry them off. A lander that reaches the top with a human becomes a mutant; lose every human and the planet explodes into a screen of them.

Getting started

  1. Insert a coin — the coin slot at the top left, or 5.
  2. Press 1 player start — the 1P button, or 1.

Strategy

  • The humans are the game. Lose all ten and the planet explodes into a far harder screen. Protecting them matters more than your own score does.
  • Catch a falling human. A lander shot high drops its captive; catching it before it lands and returning it is worth more than the lander was.
  • Smart bombs are rationed. Five at the start of a level, and one more for every 10,000 points. They are for a screen you have already lost, not a crowd you can shoot.
  • Reverse is the most-used button after fire. Turning to chase a lander across the scrolling map is most of what you do; the scanner tells you which way to face.

Scoring and settings

The setup booklet gives no point table, but it states what the machine awards:

Both are adjustable, so a machine in the wild might differ. The booklet also notes that destroying every enemy ship in a wave pays a humanoid bonus and brings on additional alien waves. From Williams’ own setup booklet.
