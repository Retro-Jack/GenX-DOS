Turbo

Sega, 1981 — the first racing game with sprite scaling, and a cabinet that moved.

System help — the arcade controls

Drive through twelve stretches of road against the clock, overtaking traffic without touching it. Reaching the next checkpoint buys more time; that is the entire structure.

Getting started

  1. Insert a coin — the coin slot at the top left, or 5.
  2. Press 1 player start — the 1P button, or 1.
  3. The real cabinet used a spinner, so hold a direction rather than tapping it — a tap is often too brief to register.

Scoring

SourcePoints
Distance travelledthe base of the score
Cars passed, at the end of each time cycle50 each

Score is tied to distance, so covering ground quickly is scored twice — once for the distance and once for the cars that puts behind you.

Strategy

  • Distance is the score. There is no lap bonus to chase; every metre counts, so a cautious run scores less even if it survives longer.
  • Passing is worth 50 apiece. Overtaking is not just avoidance — it is paid at the end of each cycle.
  • Checkpoints buy time, not points. Reaching one extends the run; that is its whole function.
  • Contact ends it. Touching traffic destroys the car outright rather than slowing you.

Arcade controls

Coin on 5, start on 1, and the six cabinet buttons on Z X C V A S. The arcade controls page covers the panel in full.
