Centipede

Atari, 1980 — Ed Logg and Dona Bailey's mushroom-field shooter, and one of the first arcade games designed with women players in mind.

System help — the arcade controls

A centipede descends through a field of mushrooms towards you. Shoot a segment and it becomes a mushroom, splitting the centipede in two. Spiders bounce through your firing zone, fleas drop new mushrooms, and scorpions poison the ones they touch — a poisoned mushroom sends the centipede charging straight down.

Getting started

  1. Insert a coin — the coin slot at the top left, or 5.
  2. Press 1 player start — the 1P button, or 1.
  3. Hold a direction rather than tapping it — this cabinet had a trackball, so movement is continuous while held.

Between two and five shooters to start, and a spare every 10,000, 12,000, 15,000 or 20,000 points depending on how the cabinet was set.

Strategy

  • Let the spider come to you. It is worth three times as much at point-blank as it is across the screen — and it eats mushrooms, which you want.
  • Mushrooms are terrain you build. Every segment you shoot becomes one. Shoot a centipede in open ground and you have made yourself a wall for later.
  • Never shoot a scorpion crossing above you. Poisoned mushrooms send the centipede diving straight down your column.
  • Heads are worth ten segments. When the centipede splits, the new head is the target.
  • Fleas only come when the field is thin. Keeping some mushrooms alive in the lower band keeps them away.

Arcade controls

Coin on 5, start on 1, and the six cabinet buttons on Z X C V A S. The arcade controls page covers the panel in full.
