Arcade · 1987 Capcom

Street Fighter

Capcom, 1987 — the first Street Fighter, five years before the one everyone remembers.

System help — the arcade controls

Ryu fights his way through a series of countries, two opponents in each, and after the first you choose the order from a map. The original cabinet had two enormous pressure-sensitive pads — how hard you hit them decided how hard Ryu hit — and this is the six-button conversion that replaced them.

Getting started

  1. Insert a coin — the coin slot at the top left, or 5.
  2. Press 1 player start — the 1P button, or 1.

Controls

Six buttons in two groups of three. The emulator’s driver reads buttons 1–3 as one control and 4–6 as another, each passing through the same pressure scale — so within a group the three buttons are the same attack at light, medium and heavy. That is Capcom’s six-button layout: punches on the first group, kicks on the second.

The pad layout here is the section’s standard six, arranged for how often a button gets pressed rather than in rows — so the punches and kicks are not laid out as neatly as a fighting cabinet would have them.

Strategy

  • This is the six-button conversion. The original cabinet had pressure pads where how hard you hit decided the strength; here the strength is which button you press.
  • You choose the order. After the first country a map lets you pick where to go next. The opponents and their patterns are fixed, so the tour is learned — but you decide which fights you take while you are still fresh.
  • Special moves are unreliable by design. The inputs are strict. Do not build a plan around landing one on demand.

No point values are documented for this cabinet — neither the emulator’s data nor any guide or manual found for it lists them.

Arcade controls

Coin on 5, start on 1, and the six cabinet buttons on Z X C V A S. The arcade controls page covers the panel in full.
