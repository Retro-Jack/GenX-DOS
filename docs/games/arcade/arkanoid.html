Arcade · 1986 Taito

Arkanoid

Taito, 1986 — the game that made Breakout a genre again, with power-ups.

System help — the arcade controls

Clear thirty-three screens of blocks with a paddle and a ball, catching capsules that widen the paddle, split the ball, or hand you a laser. The spinner made the paddle exact.

Getting started

  1. Insert a coin — the coin slot at the top left, or 5.
  2. Press 1 player start — the 1P button, or 1.
  3. The real cabinet used a spinner, so hold a direction rather than tapping it — a tap is often too brief to register.

Strategy

  • Catch the capsule you want, not the one that arrives. Capsules fall as blocks break and only one can be held. A laser taken over a widened paddle can cost more than it gains.
  • The spinner was exact. The original paddle was analogue; here it moves at a fixed rate, so plan the interception earlier than instinct suggests.
  • Angle comes from where the ball hits. The edge of the paddle returns a steeper ball. That is the only aiming there is.

These come from Taito’s manual for the home conversion; the arcade manual gives switch settings but no point table. The home version also awards a spare Vaus at 30,000, 60,000 and 90,000, which differs from the cabinet’s 20,000 and 60,000 above — so treat the block and capsule values as the game’s and the thresholds as the cabinet’s.
