The Eidolon

Activision, 1986 — Lucasfilm Games, converted for the CPC by P.A.W. Software. A Victorian inventor's machine, found in a boarded-up laboratory, that turns out to be a doorway into fractal caverns.

System help — the Amstrad CPC controls

You climb into the Eidolon — a brass-and-mahogany contraption built by a vanished 19th-century inventor — and it carries you into a cave system generated from fractals. Seven levels down, each guarded. Fight the cave's creatures with coloured fireballs, gather the power jewels they drop, and the barrier shielding that level's dragon falls away. Beat the dragon and you descend again, into caves more twisted than the last.

Getting started

  1. Click the game area to enable sound and controls.
  2. Press Fire to begin.
  3. Steer with the stick; pick a fireball colour with 1–4 and press Fire to launch it.

Controls are transcribed from Lucasfilm's own manual. This is a joystick game by design, so a USB gamepad suits it — the number keys stay on the keyboard.

The four fireballs

ColourEffect
RedThe destructive one — and the only colour you cannot safely absorb
GoldHarmless to you: catching one recharges the Eidolon's power reserve
GreenTransforms a creature it strikes into a different kind of creature
BlueAlters the flow of time; creatures struck by one are affected immediately

Reading the panel

The Eidolon's console reports everything you need: the level counter, the fireball indicators (which colours you hold, with the selected one flashing), the power jewels you've collected, a direction indicator for the way you're facing, and the minutes remaining.

Strategy

  • Make your own gold. Fire a red fireball at another red one and their energies combine into a gold — which you can then safely absorb. It's the difference between running dry and staying charged.
  • Absorb what you can. Every colour but red can be taken in to top up the power reserve, so treat stray fireballs as fuel rather than threats.
  • Three creatures, then the dragon. Each level's jewels come from its creatures; collect them and the dragon's barrier drops.
  • Mind the corners. The caves are fractal and get maze-like as you descend — it's easy to clip a wall while turning, so ease round rather than charging.
  • Watch the clock. The panel counts whole minutes remaining; exploring aimlessly costs more than it finds.
