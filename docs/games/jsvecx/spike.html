Console · GCE Vectrex

Spike

GCE, 1983 — original Vectrex platformer by Craig Lambert, and the one game in this collection that talks.

System help — the Vectrex controls

Spike must rescue his girlfriend Molly from Spud. The Vectrex draws in white lines and nothing else — every scrap of colour you see comes from the printed plastic overlay clipped over the screen, which is why the sky is blue and Spike is not. Climb ladders, jump across platforms, dodge pits, and kick or leap past the hazards. Cinema-style intros and outros — and Spike actually speaks ("Hi Mom!", "Oh no!", "All Right!") — the only game here that does.

Getting started

  1. Wait for the Vectrex BIOS intro.
  2. Press a button at the title to start.
  3. Climb, jump, and kick. Reach Molly at the top.

Controls

A USB gamepad works, and this is one of the few machines here where the left stick is the right control rather than the D-pad: the Vectrex controller was genuinely analogue in 1982, and the stick drives those same axes — how far you push it matters, not just which way. The four face buttons are the Vectrex's four: X is button 1, Y is 2, A is 3 and B is 4. The D-pad only stands in on pads that have no stick at all.

Strategy

  • Plan jumps from corners. Spike's jump arc is fixed; mid-air control is minimal. Stand at the edge before pressing jump.
  • Rocks vs. Spud. Spud appears on the upper levels. Three rocks knock him off the platform.
  • Ladder safety. Climbing ladders takes time; check for falling rocks/Spud before starting up.
  • Speech is automatic. Spike's voice ("Hi Mom!", "Oh no!") plays during the cut-scenes — there's no button for it.

Vectrex controls

All four buttons are in use. The voice is not a speech chip — the console has none, and neither does the cartridge. The machine plays samples by flicking its sound DAC between a handful of levels very fast, so the words live in the timing rather than in any dedicated hardware. Of the games here, Spike is the only one that does it.
