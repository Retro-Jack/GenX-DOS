Quarth

Konami, 1990 — a "shoot-'em-up puzzle" (クォース; arcade 1989, ported to MSX in 1990; released on the NES/Game Boy as Block Hole). Ragged clumps of blocks scroll down towards your ship at the bottom; you fire blocks up to fill them into complete solid rectangles, which then vanish — Tetris's shape-completion run in reverse, at a shooter's pace.

Your ship sits at the bottom and slides left/right. Incomplete block formations descend from the top; every shot adds one block to whatever's above you. Turn a formation into a perfect filled rectangle and the whole thing disappears for points — the bigger and more complex the rectangle, the bigger the score. Let any block touch the bottom row and it's game over. It's equal parts spatial puzzle and twitch reflex: you're mentally completing rectangles while the screen keeps falling.

Strategy

  • Think in rectangles, not blocks. A formation only clears when it forms a complete filled rectangle — scan for the missing cells and fire to fill exactly those, not just the nearest gap.
  • Bigger clears score far more. Resist clearing small 2×2s the instant you can; extending a formation into a large rectangle before completing it is worth a lot more points.
  • Watch the bottom line, not the top. The danger is the lowest block — keep the column under your ship clear so you always have somewhere safe to retreat.
  • Konami pedigree. A lesser-known Konami original from the Gradius/Parodius era — pure arcade design, no licence, all reflex; it later seeded the falling-block-shooter idea picked up by games like Magical Drop.
