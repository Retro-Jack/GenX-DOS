Missile Command

Atari, 1981 — Rob Fulop's 2600 port of Dave Theurer's 1980 arcade. Cold War paranoia in cartridge form.

Six cities are under ICBM attack. You have three anti-missile batteries with limited ammo. Aim a cursor at incoming missiles' projected paths and fire — missiles explode in a small blast that destroys anything inside it. Save the cities, ration ammo, survive waves of increasing density.

Getting started

  1. Press V (Select) to cycle game variant.
  2. Press Enter (Reset) to start.
  3. Move the cursor with the joystick; Space fires a missile at the cursor's location.

Strategy

  • Lead the missile. ICBMs travel in straight lines from top of screen to a city. Aim ahead of the missile head, not at it.
  • Stack blasts. A single explosion can take multiple missiles if their projected paths cross at the blast point. Watch the trail-splits — branching ICBMs near the apex are best caught at the split.
  • Defend the centre. Cities cluster around the central battery. Sacrifice an outer city if it saves the centre.
  • Smart bombs need close kills. They dodge your explosions, so detonate as close to them as possible — their dodge logic only triggers within a small radius.
  • End-of-wave bonus. Unused ammo and surviving cities are worth multiples; if you can clear the wave with rockets to spare, you bank serious score.

Atari 2600 controls

The 2600 port replaces the arcade trackball with a joystick — slower, less precise. Practice anticipation; you'll lose to single ICBMs at first.
