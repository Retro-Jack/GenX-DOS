Personal Computer · Commodore PET

Lunar Lander

1979 (unverified) — a real-time moon landing for the PET. Set your engine's thrust with the number keys and bring the craft down at less than 15 feet per second before the fuel runs out.

System help — the Commodore PET controls

A box of gauges in the corner shows SPEED in feet per second, HEIGHT in feet, FUEL left and elapsed TIME, beside the lunar module with its exhaust flame growing as you thrust harder. The descent runs on its own: every half second the craft falls, your thrust slows it, and that thrust burns fuel. When the fuel is gone the engine stops and you fall like a rock.

The program gives no author or date, and neither has turned up elsewhere, so the 1979 in the menu is unconfirmed.

Getting started

  1. Click the game area to enable controls.
  2. The instructions appear straight away. At DO YOU UNDERSTAND? type Y and press Return — any other answer shows them again.
  3. A five-second countdown runs, and the descent begins.

Always type something before pressing Return at a question. On the PET, Return on its own stops the program at READY. — type RUN and press Return to start again.

Controls

This emulator is keyboard-only — USB gamepads aren't supported.

KeyboardAction
0 – 9Set the engine's thrust: 0 is off, higher numbers thrust harder
5Exactly balances the moon's gravity — you keep falling at the same speed

A key sets the thrust and it stays there until you press another number; there is no need to hold it. If the speed is shown in reverse (light on dark swapped), you are going up.

The descent

At the startValue
Height52,800 ft, give or take 10,000
Falling at176 ft/s, give or take 50
Fuel2,500 units, give or take 500

Each half second burns as many units of fuel as the thrust setting. Touch down at less than 15 ft/s for CONGRATULATIONS, YOU LANDED SAFELY; any faster and you crash. DO YOU WANT TO TRY AGAIN? — type Y and Return for another go.

Tips

  • Fall freely at first. Thrust below 5 still lets you speed up, and fuel spent high up is fuel you won't have near the ground.
  • Use 5 to hold your speed. Once you're falling at a rate you like, 5 keeps it steady while you watch the height come down.
  • Brake late but firmly. A high number near the surface kills your speed quickly — but watch the fuel, because an empty tank a few hundred feet up ends the same way as no plan at all.
