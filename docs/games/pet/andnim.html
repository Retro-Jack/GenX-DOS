Android NIM

Commodore, 1979 — one of the original PET bundle games. Three rows of androids on a battlefield; you and the PET take turns removing them. Last one to remove an android loses.

System help — the Commodore PET controls

Classic Nim with PETSCII androids in three rows of seven, five, and three. On each turn, choose a row and how many androids to remove from it (must be at least one). You and the PET alternate. The player forced to take the last android loses.

Getting started

  1. Click the game area to enable sound and controls.
  2. Press any key past the title to start. Pick "Easy" or "Hard" for the PET's play strength.

Strategy (optimal Nim)

  • Misère Nim with three heaps. Unlike standard Nim, last-takes-loses inverts the endgame: leave your opponent with an odd number of single-android rows.
  • XOR-sum. Compute the bitwise XOR of all three pile sizes; if non-zero you're in a winning position. Make a move that reduces the XOR to zero.
  • Endgame inversion. When two rows are empty, take from the remaining row to leave exactly 1 — forcing the opponent to take the last.
  • The "Hard" PET plays optimally. Your only chance is forcing a win from move 1; otherwise the opening was symmetric and you lose.
