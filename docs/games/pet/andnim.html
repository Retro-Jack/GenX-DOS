Personal Computer · Commodore PET

Android NIM

1979 — by Don Dennis of Toronto. The game of Nim played against three animated executioner androids: take turns destroying the androids in three rows, and whoever gets the last one wins.

System help — the Commodore PET controls

Three rows of androids stand on the screen — seven, five and three of them — watched over by three executioners labelled A, B and C, who talk to you across the top of the screen. On your turn you choose a row and how many androids to destroy from it, and the executioners carry it out. Then the PET takes its turn. Whoever destroys the very last android wins.

Getting started

  1. Click the game area to enable controls.
  2. Do you need instructions? — press Y or N.
  3. Do you want to play first? — press Y or N.

Every answer is a single key — there is no need to press Return. Wait until a question has finished appearing before you answer; a key pressed while the androids are still talking can be lost. Take too long and they nag you.

Controls

This emulator is keyboard-only — USB gamepads aren't supported.

KeyboardAction
A B CAt WHICH ROW? — pick the row, by the executioner standing over it
1 – 9At HOW MANY ANDROIDS? — how many to destroy from that row
Y / NAnswer the yes-or-no questions

Ask for more androids than the row has left and the executioner tells you how many there are; pick an empty row and it refuses. An answer that isn't a row or a number gets YOUR ANSWER DOES NOT MAKE SENSE.

Strategy

  • This is ordinary Nim. Taking the last android wins, so there is no end-game twist to plan for.
  • Balance the rows. Write each row's count in binary and combine them with exclusive-or. If the result is not zero, some move makes it zero — make that move, and keep doing it, and you can't lose.
  • Leave matching rows. Leave two equal rows with the third empty, then copy whatever the PET takes from one row in the other, and the last android is yours.
  • Going first matters. Seven, five and three combine to a non-zero result, so the first player can always win with perfect play. Say yes to playing first.
