Uridium

Hewson (Andrew Braybrook), 1986 — skim your Manta fighter over fifteen giant Super-Dreadnoughts, strafing surface installations and dodging fighters, then make a precision landing to destroy each ship.

System help — the Commodore 64 controls

Single-player horizontal shoot-'em-up. Each level is one vast metal dreadnought (named after a metal — Chromium, Mercury, Cobalt…) scrolling beneath you. The Manta flies low over its surface, weaving between raised girders and pylons, shooting gun emplacements and waves of enemy fighters. Clear enough resistance and the "LAND NOW" prompt appears — drop into the landing strip to blow the dreadnought and advance. Braybrook's hallmark is the silky, instant-response scrolling; the Manta turns on a dime.

Getting started

  1. Click the game area to enable sound and controls.
  2. Press fire (numpad 0) at the title to launch.

Controls

GamepadKeyboardAction
D-pad ↑ / ↓Numpad 8 / 2Climb / dive (move up and down the dreadnought)
D-pad ← / →Numpad 4 / 6Brake / accelerate — full reverse flips the Manta to fly the other way
BNumpad 0 or 5Fire; also commits the landing run when "LAND NOW" shows

Strategy

  • Roll, don't barge. Pushing up or down hard makes the Manta barrel-roll — briefly invulnerable and the fastest way to dodge a fighter or a girder.
  • Girders are walls. Clipping a raised surface pylon kills you. Read the skyline ahead and flick between lanes early.
  • Reverse is a weapon. Decelerating to a full stop snaps the ship around — use it to re-strafe a row of guns rather than looping the long way.
  • Landing is the real test. The strip narrows and you must throttle right down to touch in cleanly; come in too fast and you crash on the deck you just cleared.
  • Braybrook pedigree. Same author as Paradroid and Morpheus — Uridium's metallic dreadnoughts and instant scroll set the C64 benchmark every later shmup chased.
