Ballistix

Superior Software / Acornsoft, 1989 — Peter Scott's BBC conversion of Martin Edmondson's Psyclapse original. Frantic future-sport: hose the puck into the other goal with streams of fired balls.

You control a firing arrow, not a player. Fire balls at the puck — the large red ball — to batter it into your opponent's goal; three goals wins the level. One-player mode runs 29 levels against the computer, and losing a single level terminates the game back to level 1 — the four-letter passwords the game hands out are your lifeline. Two-player is a straight fight over 30 levels. Goals score 15–85 points by quality: driven home from centre-ground beats a tap-in at the post.

Getting started

  1. At the key screen (the bleeping), press C to redefine the keys if you want — then at the title, 1 starts one-player, 2 two-player.
  2. P then a four-letter password starts at any level you've previously reached — the game shows each level's password as you earn it. They're yours to collect, so none are listed here.
  3. Mind the rebound: a puck arriving at very high velocity can bounce back out of the goal.

Strategy

  • Goal quality is the score. 15–85 points per goal — work the puck to centre-ground before the killer blow rather than scrapping at the goalmouth.
  • Never skip a password. One lost level against the computer ends the whole game — the password is the only thing standing between you and level 1.
  • Shoot the furniture. Splitters flood the pitch with your balls, the fire-stopper silences the opponent, and the RICOCHET set is worth up to 500 — sometimes the puck isn't the best target.
  • Use the ground. Accelerator arrows and magnets can carry the puck for free, tunnels reposition it, and oil slicks are handy brakes in front of your own goal.
