Holed Out

The 4th Dimension, 1989 — Gordon J. Key. The BBC's benchmark 3D golf: two 18-hole courses viewed from behind the golfer, for one to four players across four skill levels.

A full round of golf on Cone Links or Pine Isles, with water, sand traps and trees between you and par. Everything is played with three keys — Z, X and Return — through each shot's stages: pick a club, aim, then stop the moving power bar at the right moment. The skill levels decide how much the wind and your lie punish you.

Getting started

  1. From the menus, choose your options, then press 1 for Cone Links or 2 for Pine Isles. Skill levels — Beginner, Enthusiast, Amateur or Professional — decide how forgiving the wind and lies are.
  2. Each shot walks through the same three stages below. Watch the wind and distance readouts before committing.

Controls — the three stages of a shot

StageKeysWhat happens
1. ClubZ / X, then ReturnCycle through the bag, confirm your club
2. DirectionZ / X, then ReturnMove the aiming cursor, confirm the line
3. PowerReturn — or Z / XThe bar sweeps from 19% to maximum and back; Return strikes at the shown power. Striking with Z hooks the shot, X slices it

Ctrl+Q while the scoreboard is displayed abandons the round. F12 (BBC BREAK) soft-resets back to the option screens from anywhere in the game — the course menu even advertises it: BREAK..CHANGE OPTIONS.

Strategy

  • The power bar cycles — it doesn't stop at the top. If you miss the peak, let it come back round rather than taking 40% out of panic.
  • Hook and slice are tools, not just mistakes. Striking with Z or X bends the ball deliberately — the way round a tree line that's blocking the straight shot.
  • Skill level is really a wind-and-lie dial. Move up a level and the wind starts mattering and bad lies start costing distance — club up into a headwind.
  • A full 18 takes a while. Use the load/save buttons at the bottom-left of the browser window to keep a round across sessions — five slots in the save and load menus.
