Home Computer · BBC Master 128

Nevryon

The Fourth Dimension, 1990. An R-Type-style horizontal shoot-em-up that pushed the BBC hard — eight levels of parallax scrolling and multi-channel sound. Set in the First Great Space War of 2100: aliens have seized the titanium-rich colony world of Nevryon, and you fly its last-chance Cavern Fighter against them.

System help — the BBC Master controls

You fly left-to-right through eight levels, weaving past terrain and shooting alien formations and the gun structures built into the ground, roof and air. Ordinary aliens take two laser hits to kill — and every tenth kill drops a bonus pod in front of you (roughly where the tenth alien died, so it can land awkwardly over scenery). Touch a pod to collect it: pods upgrade your weapons in a fixed order, and a fully-armed fighter is what you need to dismantle the fortress that guards the end of each level. Any contact with anything alien drains a unit of energy, so this is as much about weaving as shooting.

Getting started

  1. Page through the instruction pages with Space, or press Esc to skip straight to the title screen.
  2. Set options on the title screen with the function keys (skill, speed, resolution…), then press Space to start on level 1 — or enter a password first to begin a later scenario.
  3. Hold Return for constant fire, touch bonus pods to pick up weapons, and pull your gun droid back with Space when it drifts out of position.

Weapons (collect the bonus pods)

Every tenth alien killed drops a pod; touch it to collect. Weapons arrive in this order and, except Rapid Fire, each needs two pods. Whatever you're carrying lasts until the end of the level or until you're killed.

Strategy

  • Hold fire, always. Constant fire costs nothing — keep Return down and put your attention on positioning and dodging.
  • Line up the tenth kill. Pods spawn where the tenth alien dies, so time that shot away from the scenery or the pod can land somewhere unreachable.
  • Save missiles for the fortress. Each level ends at a fortress you dismantle piece by piece — missiles do it far faster, so try to reach it fully armed.
  • Beat the force fields. Force-field generators come in pairs — shoot one (top or bottom) or thread the gap rather than flying into the field.
  • Passwords vs. credits. Finishing a scenario prints a password that lets you skip it in a later game — handy for practising the back half. But use a password and you forfeit your three continue credits; a true clear is all eight levels in a single run, no passwords, no continues.
