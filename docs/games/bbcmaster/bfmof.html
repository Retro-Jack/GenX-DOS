By Fair Means or Foul

Superior Software / Acornsoft, 1988 — Michael & Terry Simpson. Boxing where the title is the tactic: punch clean while the referee's watching, and rather less clean when he isn't.

Defeat each opposing boxer inside 15 rounds — or your 5 chances (lives) run out. Land blows to drain the energy bar: a knock-out just ends the round, and energy is restored for the next one, so the war is really over chances. Fouls — the head butt, knee, kick and groin punch — do the damage, but only pay when the referee isn't looking. Beat six increasingly hard opponents for the world title, then defend it. Two-player versus supported.

Getting started

  1. F1 starts a one-player game, F2 two-player. This Master version asks you to name your boxer — type a name (or take the default) and press Space to start; in two-player, pick characters with the cursor keys and Return first.
  2. F3 / F4 cycle the control option (joystick/keyboard layouts) for player 1 / player 2.
  3. Watch your corner silhouette. The boxer silhouettes in the top corners show your remaining chances, and their colour is the foul-safety meter: green = the ref won't see it, amber = risky, red = he's watching. Get caught fouling and a chance is gone.

Strategy

  • Green means go. Foul on green, never on red — a seen foul costs a chance, and chances are the actual scoreboard: lose 5 and it's over, whatever the rounds say.
  • Knock-downs don't finish it. Emptying his bar only ends the round and everyone's energy refills — you win by attrition of chances across up to 15 rounds.
  • Don't lean on the clinch. Get too close and the boxers automatically hold and swap sides of the ring; repeat it too often and you risk forfeiting a chance.
  • Guards are free. High and low guard work with or without Fire held — when in doubt, cover up and wait for the silhouette to turn green.
