Bonecruncher

Superior Software, 1987 — Andreas Kemnitz & Martyn Howard. Boulder Dash-style puzzler with a gleefully morbid premise: you run a bathing company, and real soap is made from bones.

System help — the BBC Master controls

You are Bono, a friendly green dragon, and the monsters bathing in your moat expect luxury skeleton soap. Collect the skeletons littered around the castle, boil them in your cauldron — five skeletons to a bar — and keep the customers lathered across 22 increasingly hazardous chambers.

Getting started

  1. At the controls screen, press Space to continue (or Return to skip the loading screen). Press P if you have a password to skip ahead; otherwise just play on from chamber one.
  2. Gather skeletons (you can carry up to nine), drop five in the cauldron for a bar of soap, and deliver the bars to the monsters — five bars finishes the chamber.
  3. Completing a chamber shows you its password. Finish all 22 without using one and the game awards you a certificate.

Strategy

  • Know your monsters. The green ones kill on contact — trap them with glooks, or let Fozzy hold them. A monster crushed under a glook becomes another skeleton for the pot.
  • Fozzy follows the right-hand wall, spiders follow the left. Spiders are lethal to Bono (and eat your skeletons) but can't hurt Fozzy — and Fozzy has only three lives and limited energy, so don't spend him cheaply.
  • Glooks follow the smell of soap. They're drawn to you once you're carrying bars, but they can be pushed aside — and used as movable walls.
  • Watch the floor. Trapdoors and hidden volcanoes are invisible until sprung, doors need keys (you can hold nine), and earth only clears where you dig it.
  • Inventory caps at nine of everything — skeletons, soap and keys. Plan cauldron trips rather than hoarding.
