Phantasy Star

Sega, 1988. The landmark science-fantasy RPG: Alis avenges her brother Nero across the three planets of Algol, with a talking cat, a warrior, a wizard — and first-person 3D dungeons years ahead of their time.

One of the most ambitious 8-bit games ever shipped: a top-down overworld across Palma, Motavia and Dezoris, towns full of NPCs worth interrogating, turn-based battles you can sometimes talk your way through, and smooth-scrolling first-person dungeons that expect you to keep a map. This is a long campaign — and the saving works properly here.

Getting started

  1. Press Z or X at the title, start a new quest, and name your save.
  2. Move with the arrows; the buttons open and confirm the command menus (talk, search, items, magic) — everything in Phantasy Star flows through them.
  3. Talk to everyone. Directions, passwords and plot hinges hide in townsfolk chatter.

Saving — read this one

Phantasy Star's own battery-backup save (from the church/save menus) persists in your browser between sessions — the cartridge RAM is stored locally, exactly like a real battery cart. The GenX save/load buttons (bottom-left, five slots) snapshot the whole machine on top of that. Belt and braces: use the in-game save as your real progress, and the slots for mid-dungeon insurance.

Strategy

  • Map the dungeons. The 3D corridors were designed for graph paper, and graph paper still works. (Each floor is a modest grid — count your steps.)
  • Talking is a battle option. Some encounters can be negotiated instead of fought — worth trying when a fight looks losing.
  • The party assembles slowly. Odin the warrior, Myau the cat and Noah the wizard each join through side-quests of their own — progress stalls until you find them, so chase every NPC lead.
  • Vehicles unlock the map. The Landrover, Hovercraft and Ice Digger open terrain no boots can cross; they're plot-critical purchases, not conveniences.
  • Grind near churches. Experience and meseta come faster when a revive is a short walk away.
