Tunnels of Doom

Texas Instruments (Kevin Kenney, John Plaster), 1982 — Solid State Software cart. The 4A's signature dungeon-crawler: an early CRPG that mixes a top-down dungeon-exploration view with first-person combat encounters. Party of four heroes, ten dungeon levels, two pre-loaded quest scenarios.

Generate a party (Fighter/Wizard/Thief/etc.), enter the dungeon, navigate top-down. When you collide with a monster, the screen switches to a tactical 4-square first-person view: each character takes a turn to move, attack, negotiate, or use an item. Solve the scenario — "Pennies and Prizes" (rescue), "Quest for the King" (escort) — by reaching the lowest dungeon level and recovering the goal.

Getting started

  1. Press 2 at the TI title.
  2. Choose scenario, then generate party stats — re-roll until you're happy.

Controls

KeyAction
↑ ↓ ← →Move one step (move into an adjacent enemy to attack in melee)
EnterPass / confirm when no enemy is adjacent
W (combat)Change weapon
F (combat)Fire a ranged weapon — a cursor appears; aim with the arrows, then Space
N (combat)Negotiate — try to avoid the fight
U (combat)Use a magic item

Strategy

  • Map the dungeon as you go. The cart has no auto-map; the top-down view shows only the room you're in. Pencil a paper map per level.
  • Strike, then drop back. Combat is turn-based; fire a ranged weapon (F) or use a magic item (U), then step back with the arrows so melee enemies can't reach.
  • Save vials. Health potions can't be bought mid-dungeon; ration them for boss fights only.
  • Quest scenarios are different. "Pennies and Prizes" is rescue-focused; "Quest for the King" is escort + retrieve — each rewards different party compositions.
  • An early CRPG benchmark. The first-person combat view predates Wizardry's NES port; CRPG historians cite Tunnels as a key bridge from Dungeons & Dragons modules into computer RPGs.
