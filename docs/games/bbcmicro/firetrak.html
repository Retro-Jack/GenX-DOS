Firetrack

Electric Dreams, 1987 — Nick Pelling (who also wrote Frak!). A vertically-scrolling shooter built to show what the BBC could really do: smooth flicker-free scrolling and colour tricks that make the Beeb's eight-colour palette look far wider than it is.

System help — the BBC Micro controls

You fly a lone fighter up a series of scrolling worlds, shooting the gun emplacements and craft that rise to meet you. The landscape itself is the enemy as much as the ships are — the track narrows, turrets line the walls, and your only answer is accurate shooting and a steady line.

Getting started

  1. Let the game load, then start when prompted — the ship begins at the bottom of the first world.
  2. Shoot everything that emerges from the scenery; the gun emplacements along the track fire back and are worth clearing before they line you up.
  3. Hold the fire key for fast fire — Firetrack's shooting is meant to be held down, not tapped.

Controls

Firetrack offers two keyboard layouts, both live at once — use whichever suits. The BBC's *: and / ? keys aren't where a PC keyboard puts them — this emulator maps the BBC *: key to your PC apostrophe ' and the BBC / ? key to /.

The alternative layout uses Caps Lock left, Ctrl right, ] up, ' down and the ↑ cursor key for fast fire. (Those last two are the BBC's [ and : keys, which sit one key to the left of where a PC prints them.)

A USB gamepad flies it as-is — the d-pad steers and A is fast fire, because Firetrack's main layout is exactly the pad's default keys. No setup, no remap.

Strategy

  • Hold fire, don't tap. The fire key is a fast fire — holding it gives the stream of shots the game is balanced around.
  • Clear the wall turrets first. Emplacements along the track keep firing as you climb past; taking them early costs less than dodging them later.
  • Fly the gaps, not the middle. As the track narrows, the safe line is usually hugging one side — commit to a side rather than drifting across the centre.
  • Start further in once you know it. F6–F8 pick a starting world, so you can practise a later stage without replaying the first every time.
  • Snapshot before a new world. Firetrack has no in-game save, so use the save/load buttons at the bottom-left of the browser window — five slots — to bank your progress.
