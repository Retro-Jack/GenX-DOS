Citadel

Superior Software, 1985 — Michael Jakobsen. Sprawling arcade-adventure-platformer with ~100 interconnected rooms; one of the BBC's best-loved exploration games.

Find the five hidden crystals scattered across the maze and return them to their rightful place to open the way into the citadel. Rooms hold keys, food, scrolls, and helpful objects — and creatures that drain energy on contact. Find the right object for the right obstacle: the puzzle is half platforming, half adventure inventory.

Getting started

  1. At the title, the "select which key layout" screen appears — press 1–4 to choose one of four keyboard layouts, or press Fire to use a joystick.
  2. You start outside the castle. Walk through the door on the right and collect everything you can pick up — most early rooms have a single key item.
  3. You carry one object at a time; drop the current item by walking over a new one. Choose carefully.

Controls

Citadel lets you pick from four keyboard layouts at the start. Layout 1 (the default) is shown below; layouts 2–4 swap in alternatives such as Caps/Ctrl, Delete/Copy or C/V. The BBC's * and ? keys aren't where a PC keyboard puts them — this emulator maps the BBC *: key to your PC apostrophe ' and the BBC / ? key to /, so press those for up/down.

Strategy

  • Map as you go. Citadel is huge and has no in-game map. A pen and paper grid is the original walkthrough.
  • Energy is the only timer. No clock — but every monster hit chips away at the bar. Always know where the nearest apple is.
  • Object swaps cost progress. If you drop the key and walk away, the room's enemies usually take it back. Carry the key all the way to the door.
  • Dead-end rooms are clues. If you've cleared everywhere reachable and a room remains sealed, you're missing an object — not a jump.
  • Snapshot before risky rooms. Citadel has no in-game save, so use the save/load buttons at the bottom-left of the browser window — five slots — to bank your progress before a tricky platform run.
