Repton

Superior Software, 1985 — Tim Tyler. Boulder-Dash-inspired puzzle that became Superior's flagship; 12 screens, distinctive musical jingle on every diamond.

Repton burrows through dirt collecting diamonds and avoiding falling rocks, hostile creatures, and his own claustrophobic geometry. Each of the 12 screens has a fixed layout: dig the right tunnels in the right order to reach every diamond without trapping yourself or letting a boulder hit you.

Getting started

  1. Press Space at the title to start playing (you begin on Screen A).
  2. To resume further in, press P and enter a screen password — the title/status screen shows the current screen's password.
  3. Eat all the diamonds on a screen to open the exit. Time bonus depends on how fast you do it.

Controls

Repton's up/down are the BBC * and ? keys, which aren't where a PC keyboard puts them — this emulator maps the BBC *: key to your PC apostrophe ' and the BBC / ? key to /, so press those.

Strategy

  • Plan the diamond order, not the route. The path you dig becomes permanent geometry — get a diamond order wrong and you can seal off the rest of the screen.
  • Push boulders sideways only when grounded. Repton can push a single boulder horizontally if the square behind it is empty. Two stacked boulders pin you.
  • Eggs are timed. Crack them on purpose by dropping a boulder before they hatch.
  • Diagonal moves don't exist. Every step is one square — long diagonals across a screen mean a lot of dirt.
  • Keep the screen password. Repton remembers progress by password, not a save file — each screen's password appears on the title/status screen, so jot it down and press P to jump straight back there next time.
