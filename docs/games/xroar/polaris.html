Polaris

Device Oriented Games for Tandy, 1981 — six islands, three submarines and twenty-seven missiles against a sky that never stops falling. Aim the crosshairs, fire early, and hope your maths was right.

System help — the Tandy CoCo controls

Enemy missiles rain on your six islands. You cannot move and you cannot hide — you have three submarines dug in along the sea floor, nine missiles apiece, and a pair of crosshairs you steer with the joystick. Your missile explodes where the sights are, so you aim at where the enemy is going to be, not where it is. Each level falls faster and thicker, and the screen changes colour as it goes to make the incoming harder to pick out.

Getting started

  1. The cartridge auto-starts; click the game area to enable sound and controls.
  2. Press 1 or 2 to choose the number of players.
  3. Steer the crosshairs with the joystick, then fire with Z, X or C — each key launches from a different submarine.

The aiming is on the joystick and the firing is on the keyboard, which is how the game was made — a gamepad steers the sights, but the launch keys stay under your other hand.

Without a gamepad the cursor keys stand in for the stick. With one plugged in the left stick aims, and it moves the sights faster the further you push it — the game reads how far the stick is over, not merely which way. A gamepad only works in Firefox; Chromium browsers will not hand one to this page, and it says so on screen if you are in one.

Scoring

Enemy missile5

Everything is multiplied by the level number shown at the top of the screen — 1X to begin with, then 2X, 3X and upward. At 2,000 points you are given a spare island, held in reserve until one of yours is destroyed.

Strategy

  • Lead the target. Your missile takes time to reach the sights, so aim at the point the enemy will occupy when it arrives. Firing where something already is wastes the shot.
  • Fire from the nearest submarine. The three launch keys pick which sub fires, and a missile from the far side of the map takes noticeably longer to get there.
  • Explosions wrap around the screen edges. A blast near the left edge also appears at the right, so one missile can take out two attackers sitting on opposite sides.
  • Watch the smart bombs. They are worth four times an ordinary missile, but they steer around your explosions — lead them further than feels sensible.
  • Leftover missiles are points. Only one apiece, but a clean round with a full rack beats a scrappy one, and running a submarine dry mid-wave leaves you reaching for a slower launcher.
