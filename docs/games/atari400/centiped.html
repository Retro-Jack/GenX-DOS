Centipede

Atari, 1982 — home port of Dona Bailey and Ed Logg's 1981 arcade. The arcade was famous for its trackball; the Atari 8-bit version uses joystick. One of the first arcade games co-designed by a woman (Bailey).

Mushroom-shooter from the bottom of a mushroom-filled grid. A centipede snakes down through the mushrooms toward you; when shot, each hit segment becomes a mushroom and the centipede splits. Spiders, fleas, and scorpions add complications. Survive each wave to advance.

Getting started

  1. Press F4 Start.
  2. Move along the bottom strip with the joystick.
  3. Fire upward to hit centipede segments and mushrooms.
  4. Stay alert for spiders (jumping diagonally) and fleas (dropping straight).

Strategy

  • Centre lane control. Keep the centre of the bottom strip clear of mushrooms; the centipede dives faster through dense fields.
  • Head shots first. Killing the head changes the next-back segment into the new head. Easier to chain than mid-body shots.
  • Poison mushrooms drop the centipede vertically. If the centipede touches a poisoned mushroom it dives straight down — useful for setting up a kill zone.
  • Spider priority. Close-range spider = 900 points but very risky. Long-range spiders = safer 300.
  • Flea creates mushrooms as it drops. Don't ignore them — they replenish the field.

Atari 400 controls

Joystick + fire. The Atari 8-bit Centipede port was praised at launch for matching arcade colour and animation despite running on home hardware.
